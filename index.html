    Frequently asked questions

    Start with the question you have right now.

    How do I apply for SSI for my disabled child?

    You can apply online at ssa.gov, by phone, or in person — but don't rush the application. Gather your child's medical records, school records, and a list of every doctor first, because an incomplete application is a common reason families wait months just to be denied. My Childhood SSI guide walks through the whole application step by step.

    How can I get more IHSS hours?

    Most families are under-assessed because they don't know how to document their child's needs in the language IHSS uses. Keep a log for two weeks of everything you help with — every prompt, every reminder, every nighttime check — and bring it to the assessment. My IHSS Hours guide shows you exactly what to track and say.

    Can my child save money and still keep SSI?

    Yes — through an ABLE account. Normally SSI cuts off if countable resources go over $2,000, but money in an ABLE account (up to $100,000) doesn't count. It's the single most underused tool I see. My ABLE Accounts guide explains how to open one and use it without risking benefits.

    What happens to my child's disability benefits when they turn 18?

    Everything gets redetermined under the adult rules, which are completely different — and most families don't find out until the letter arrives. Start preparing at 17: understand the adult disability standard, the SSI income rules, and whether Disabled Adult Child benefits apply. My Disabled Adult Child Benefits guide covers the transition.

    Where do I even start? This system is brand new to me.

    Start with one thing, not everything. Pick the single most urgent need — usually the application or an appeal deadline — and learn just that piece. Then the next. My Caregiver's Guide is the map for the whole journey, one step at a time.

    More good to know

    Who is this guidance for?

    It is for disabled people, parents, caregivers, representative payees, conservators, and family members who want a clearer way to understand benefit processes and prepare for the next step.

    Is Anchor & Able Guide Co. a law firm?

    No. Anchor & Able Guide Co. provides general educational information and practical process guidance, not legal advice or legal representation.

    Does this replace official agency guidance?

    No. Program rules can change and individual facts matter. Always confirm current requirements with the administering agency or a qualified professional before making a financial or legal decision.

    What should I do with an urgent deadline?

    Read the notice for appeal or response instructions, keep a copy, and contact the agency or an appropriate legal-services provider promptly. Do not wait for general educational content when a deadline is running.
